# Model validation

Generated by `src/model.py` from `data/clean/flex_clean.csv` and `data/clean/exclusions.csv`. scikit-learn 1.9.1, default settings; the only searches are 5 ridge penalties and the Gaussian-process kernel fit.

## Setup

- Targets: `burning_rate_k` (unit unconfirmed) and `d_ext_over_d0`.
- Features, "base": fuel (as is_heptane), o2, pressure_atm, co2, he, d0_mm. The second feature set adds o2_base and diluent_frac, which are computed from the same inputs.
- Never used as features: burn_time_s, d_ext_mm, test_end, the other target.
- Rows: all 274 tests minus those listed per target in `exclusions.csv` (burning_rate_k: 17 excluded, 257 used; d_ext_over_d0: 86 excluded, 188 used).
- 90% interval: Gaussian process, mean +/- 1.645 x predictive standard deviation. Other models, mean +/- the 90th percentile of absolute out-of-fold residuals on the training data.
- Coverage is the share of held-out tests whose measured value falls inside the interval. The aim is 90%.

## Honesty test in plain words (chosen models)

Burning rate: when the model has never seen helium, its error on the helium tests is 0.137, which is 2.4 times its grouped-CV error on the same tests (0.057) and no better than predicting the mean (0.142); only 45% of the measured values fall inside the 90% interval. Holding out CO2 is milder: 1.6 times worse (0.057 against 0.036) with 92% coverage. In plain words, the burning-rate model does not transfer to a diluent it has not seen; helium raises the burning rate where CO2 lowers it, and nothing in the inputs tells the model that in advance. Extinction ratio: 1.2 times worse without helium and 1.4 times worse without CO2, still well ahead of the mean baseline, with coverage of 79% and 86%. Removing the k_outlier row changes the burning-rate MAE from 0.048 to 0.046 and nothing else of note.

## Is d_ext_over_d0 too noisy to model?

No, it can be modelled, but only coarsely. With 188 tests the Gaussian process reaches MAE 0.086 and R2 0.77 in grouped CV, against MAE 0.207 for the mean baseline, so there is real signal. The 90% interval is about 0.36 wide on a quantity that only spans 0.04 to 1.00: the model can say "far from the limit", "in between" or "close to the limit", not much finer. Coverage also drops to 79-86% when a whole diluent is held out, and 8 tests with extinction diameter printed as 0 are left out until their meaning is confirmed.

## Chosen models

### burning_rate_k: Gaussian process, features: base

The Gaussian process has the lowest grouped-CV error (MAE 0.048 against 0.107 for the mean baseline) with 91% coverage from its own uncertainty, and it degrades least when CO2 is held out. The base features are kept because adding o2_base and diluent_frac does not improve grouped CV (MAE 0.047) and makes the held-out-CO2 error almost three times larger (0.155 against 0.057).

Saved to `models/burning_rate_k.joblib`, trained on 257 tests (including the k_outlier row, test 130).

Fitted kernel (inputs standardised, order is_heptane, o2_mole_frac, pressure_atm, co2_mole_frac, he_mole_frac, d0_mm): `1.68**2 * RBF(length_scale=[2.12, 3.56, 4.63, 5.19, 6.53, 2.82]) + WhiteKernel(noise_level=0.196)`

### d_ext_over_d0: Gaussian process, features: base

The Gaussian process is clearly ahead in grouped CV (MAE 0.086, R2 0.77, against 0.114 and 0.61 for gradient boosting) with the narrowest interval at 91% coverage, and it is also the best model in both leave-one-diluent-out tests. The base features are kept because the two extra features change nothing (MAE 0.086 either way) and the simpler input set is easier to explain.

## Limits

- Among diluted tests O2 and diluent fraction move together (see `reports/eda.md`, section A), so the models cannot attribute an effect to one or the other inside a pressure band.
- Above 900 mmHg there are 9 tests, all at CO2 0.70; between the three pressure groups there are none.
- CO2 and He are never mixed. `predict` returns `in_range = False` for such a query.
- These are single fuel droplets. Nothing here predicts a cabin fire.
